# Gap Analysis: mrc-psych-lake (web) vs SPMM Source PDF
## Child & Adolescent Psychiatry — What's Missing, From an Exam Standpoint

**Purpose of this document:** This compares every fact, table, and heading in the single source
book for this topic — `resources/paper-b/books/10-child-psychiatry.pdf` (44 pages), extracted
verbatim into `content/notes/child-psychiatry.json` (14 NoteBlocks, one per numbered heading) —
against what currently renders on the live Child & Adolescent Psychiatry study-guide page
(`content/study-guides/child-psychiatry.json`, 20 sections). Unlike the Adult Psychiatry audit that
triggered this exercise, this guide is **not** thin: every one of the book's 11 numbered top-level
headings (Adult mental illness and children; Negative life events; ADHD; Conduct disorder/ODD;
Mood and psychotic disorders; Anxiety disorders; Other anxiety-related disorders; Developmental
disorders; Learning and communication disorders; Tic disorders; Miscellaneous topics) has a
corresponding, densely detailed section on the page, and every named study/trial found in the book
(MTA, TADS, POTS, Ontario Child Health Survey, Isle of Wight study, Romanian Adoptees Study, WHO
World Mental Health Survey/Kessler 2010, Rutter 1978, Moffitt 2002, Zoccolillo 1992, Young et al
2011, Farrington's Cambridge study as an invented-mnemonic aid) is present and cited. Every
comparison table in the book (school refusers vs truants; completed suicide vs non-fatal DSH) is
reproduced as a `comparison` block, and the guide adds several of its own well-sourced tables
(PDD comparison, tic-disorder comparison, ADHD drug classes, attachment styles, learning-disorder
prevalence) that go beyond straight paraphrase without introducing anything unsourced — spot-checks
of figures that looked suspicious (Asperger's "9:1" sex ratio, "90% dry at night by age 5") both
resolved to genuine question-bank citations, not fabrications. The 55 `gap` blocks already present
correctly and transparently flag question-bank-only facts that have no book coverage (e.g.
NICE 2018 methylphenidate-first-line guidance, structural-heart-disease stimulant contraindication,
eating disorders/Down syndrome genetics/sleep as a dedicated "question-bank-only" section) rather
than silently omitting them.

That said, a systematic block-by-block re-read of all 14 NoteBlocks against the guide did surface a
handful of concrete, verified-absent gaps — mostly whole named lists/citations that never made it
onto the page rather than paraphrase drift. Each is stated below with its literal book wording and
page.

## Section-by-Section Gap Findings

### 1. Adult Mental Illness and Children (book p.2)
**Status: fully covered —** Rutter's three mediators (direct exposure, altered
interpersonal/parenting capacity, mediator variables) and the full five-stage
maternal-depression-effects table (prenatal → infant → toddler → school-age → adolescent) are
present and match the book verbatim, with the Murray & Cooper (1997) citation intact. No gaps
found.

### 2. Negative Life Events During Development — Child Abuse & Neglect (book pp.3–5)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing one substantial checklist —**
- **The book's entire "Signs of possible abuse" checklist (book p.4) — five separate named
  categories — is reduced on the page to a single EMI trap citing only three cherry-picked items.**
  The book gives five full bulleted lists under: *Signs of possible physical abuse* (unexplained
  recurrent injuries/bruises/burns, improbable excuses, refusal to discuss injuries, untreated
  injuries or delay in reporting, excessive physical punishment); *Signs of possible physical
  neglect* (constant hunger, poor personal hygiene, constant tiredness, poor state of clothing,
  frequent lateness/non-attendance at school, untreated medical problems); *Signs of possible
  non-organic failure to thrive* (significant lack of growth, weight loss and hair loss, poor
  skin/muscle tone, circulatory disorders); *Signs of possible emotional abuse* (low self-esteem,
  sudden speech disorder, rocking/head-banging, self-mutilation); and *Signs of possible sexual
  abuse*, split into behavioural signs (lack of trust or over-familiarity with adults, fear of a
  particular individual, social isolation, sleep disturbances, running away from home, girls taking
  over the mothering role, unusual interest in genitals, inappropriate expressions of affection,
  developmental regression, over-sexualised behaviour) and physical/medical signs (bruises/
  scratches to thighs or genital area, itch/soreness/discharge/unexplained bleeding, pain on
  urination or recurrent UTI, recurrent vaginal infection, venereal disease, stained underwear,
  discomfort walking/sitting, pregnancy with reluctance to name the father). The **non-organic
  failure-to-thrive category is entirely absent from the guide** — it doesn't appear as its own
  concept anywhere on the page, only "failure to thrive" mentioned once in passing as a maternal-
  depression effect on infants (a different context). This whole checklist is exactly the kind of
  content an EMI question stem draws its "match the sign to the abuse type" options from, and right
  now only the three items already pulled into the existing trap block would be recognisable.
- Minor: the book's child-abuse risk-factor list also includes "chronic illness" as a child-side
  risk factor and states "the risk of child abuse increases in families with many children" (p.5)
  — both dropped from the guide's otherwise-thorough risk-factor paragraph, which keeps
  prematurity/congenital malformation/intellectual disability/difficult temperament but not these
  two.

### 3. Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (book pp.6–10)
**Status: fully covered, one figure missing —**
- **"Previous research shows that ADHD occurs in head injury in 25% [of] cases"** (book p.7) is
  dropped. The guide keeps the more clinically important caveat that followed it (the BMJ
  retrospective cohort showing head injury before age 2 is not causal, and may instead be a
  "marker" for later diagnosis) but drops this specific recall figure, which is exactly the kind of
  bare percentage MRCPsych SBA distractors are built from.
- Everything else — DSM-5 criteria changes, both DSM-IV/ICD-10 prevalence figures, the full
  genetics/neuroimaging/neurotransmitter aetiology, the MTA study, the complete stimulant/
  atomoxetine/TCA/alpha-2-agonist/antipsychotic drug-class table with adverse effects, and the
  five MCQ traps — is present and numerically verified against the book (79%/32% MZ/DZ
  concordance, 15% still meeting criteria at 25, 50–80% comorbidity, etc. all match exactly).

### 4. Disorders of Childhood Conduct — CD & ODD (book pp.11–14)
**Status: fully covered —** both DSM-5/ICD-10 diagnostic thresholds, the Ontario Child Health
Survey and Rutter (1978) risk-factor lists, the full protective-factors and poor-prognostic-factors
tables, functional family therapy and multisystemic therapy (including session counts, caseload
sizes, and the ~50% reoffending-reduction figure for both), and the ODD-specific epidemiology
(2–5% prevalence, 25% asymptomatic later) are all present and numerically correct, plus three
well-targeted MCQ traps (genetic-loading contrast with ADHD, CSF vs plasma serotonin, MST vs FFT
phrase-matching). No gaps found.

### 5. Early Onset Mood and Psychotic Disorders (book pp.15–21)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing two named facts and one whole diagnostic term —**
- **"Dysthymic disorder is a chronic condition with fewer symptoms than major depression, but
  lasts a minimum of one year"** (book p.16) does not appear anywhere in the guide — searched for
  "dysthymic" in every block, zero matches. This is a distinct diagnostic entity from major
  depressive disorder and is exactly the kind of "which disorder is this describing" SBA stem the
  exam uses.
- **"12% of adolescent deaths are due to suicide" (Hawton 1986)** (book p.17) is missing. The guide
  keeps "suicide is the 3rd leading cause of adolescent death" but drops this specific percentage
  and its citation.
- **The attempters-vs-completers psychiatric-disorder distinction is missing entirely**: book p.17
  states "Few adolescents who make suicide attempts actually have psychiatric disorder and serious
  suicidal intent is low. In contrast, almost all children and teenagers who commit suicide suffer
  from a psychiatric disorder at the time of death — mood disorder, alcohol/substance abuse,
  anxiety disorders, or conduct disorders." The guide's "Completed suicide vs non-fatal DSH"
  comparison table covers incidence trend/sex/age/social class/childhood history/precipitants/
  family history/setting, but never this specific and very testable psychiatric-comorbidity
  contrast between the two groups.
- Everything else — bipolar/COS epidemiology and comorbidity figures, the TADS trial numbers
  (43/35/71/61%, remission 37% vs 20%), the NICE stepped-care ladder, fluoxetine/paroxetine
  prescribing detail, and the five bipolar/COS MCQ traps — is present and numerically verified.

### 6. Anxiety Disorders — General Features & Separation Anxiety (book pp.22–26)
**Status: fully covered, one figure missing —**
- **"Persistent mutism affects less than 1 per 1000 children"** (book p.25) is a second, more
  specific prevalence figure for selective mutism beyond the "3–8/10,000 in the UK" figure the
  guide already states — currently only the broader figure appears, not this narrower "persistent"
  cutoff.
- All prevalence-by-disorder figures (SAD 3.5%/0.8%, GAD ~4%, specific phobia ~10%, social phobia
  1%/5–15%, panic disorder 3–6%), the age-related presentation pattern, the comorbidity
  cross-pattern between SAD/GAD/social phobia, and the full anxiety-disorders key-characteristics
  summary (Lewis's textbook) are present and correct, albeit rendered as prose rather than the
  book's own two-column table format — a cosmetic, not fidelity, difference.

### 7. Other Anxiety-Related Disorders — PTSD, OCD, PANDAS, Elimination Disorders (book pp.27–31)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing one named audit finding —**
- **Silver (1996)'s encopresis family-pattern audit finding is entirely absent.** Book p.31: "Family
  patterns such as an unhappy child in a family with clear ongoing difficulties [abuse, domestic
  violence], recent acute stress in the family [death, birth of sibling etc.], and over tolerant
  parents have been found in half the families studied in an audit [Silver, 1996]." The guide's
  encopresis paragraph covers sexual-abuse association, maternal hostility, and harsh/punitive
  parenting, but this specific named citation with its "half the families" figure and the distinct
  risk factors (family stress events, over-tolerant parenting) is missing.
- PTSD (trauma classification Type 1/2, DSM-5 threshold changes, trauma-focused CBT), OCD
  (aetiology, comorbidity, POTS trial, treatment ladder), PANDAS/Sydenham's chorea (GABHS odds
  ratios 3.1/12.1, Singer et al's >50%/11% figures, sawtooth course), and enuresis (developmental
  norms table, bell-and-pad >60% effectiveness, desmopressin/imipramine/reboxetine/oxybutynin
  detail, Hirschsprung's-disease trap) are all present and numerically verified — including a
  correctly-flagged internal inconsistency in the book's own OCD twin-concordance sentence.

### 8. Developmental Disorders — Autism Spectrum, Asperger's, Rett's, CDD (book pp.32–36)
**Status: fully covered —** the DSM-IV/DSM-5 PDD reclassification, the full genetics section
(sibling recurrence 2–8%, twin concordance figures, Fragile X and tuberous sclerosis rates,
chromosome linkage), the MRI/neuropathology findings (ventricular enlargement, vermal lobule VI/VII
hypoplasia, Purkinje cell loss, lobe-specific volume changes), the long-term outcome breakdown
(12/10/19/46/12%), Applied Behavioural Analysis and TEACCH programme details, and the Asperger's/
Rett's/CDD paragraphs (including the correctly-cited "9:1" Asperger's sex ratio, which traces to a
question-bank EMI rather than the book, and is legitimately dual-sourced in the PDD comparison
table) are all present and numerically correct. No gaps found.

### 9. Learning and Communication Disorders (book pp.37–39)
**Status: fully covered —** all five specific learning/communication disorder prevalence and
sex-ratio figures (dyslexia 4%/4:1, written expression 2–8%/3:1, mathematics disorder up to 6%/
female predominance, expressive language 3–5%/2–3:1, stuttering ~1%/3:1), the WORD/TOWRE/WISC
assessment tools, and the full aetiology/management detail for each subtype are present and match
the book. No gaps found.

### 10. Tic Disorders — Tourette Syndrome & Related (book pp.40–41)
**Status: fully covered —** Tourette diagnostic criteria, the GABHS odds-ratio figures, the
MZ/DZ twin concordance (50%/10%), the clonidine-first/guanfacine-30%-reduction management ladder,
and the transient/chronic/Tourette's/PANDAS four-way comparison table are all present and
numerically correct. No gaps found.

### 11. Miscellaneous Topics — Substance Misuse, Rating Scales, Inpatient Care (book pp.42–44)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing one whole named citation —**
- **Dalton et al. (1989)'s seven factors against child psychiatric hospitalisation are entirely
  missing.** Book p.44 lists them explicitly: disruption of the child's family/community
  relationships; expenses incurred by family and health services; reinforcement of parental denial
  or guilt; sibling's confused/distorted perception of the issues treated; removal from the
  continuum of education; predictable noxious stigmata and labeling; potential for unresolved
  transference and institutionalisation. The guide's "Rating Scales & Assessment Tools" section
  captures **Hersov (1994)'s** five admission criteria in full but drops this preceding, equally
  citable, named list entirely — a natural source for an "except" question ("all of the following
  are cited by Dalton et al. as arguments against inpatient admission except...").
- Substance-misuse figures (alcohol ~30% of 13-year-olds, cannabis ~5% of 11–16-year-olds, heroin
  IV-conversion ≥15%, cocaine 2.4%/0.5% US/UK, methamphetamine 4.7%→2.5%, steroid use 4–12% US
  males) and the full rating-scales roster (Connor's, ADI-R, WISC, SDQ, CBCL, CAFAS, CDI, BDI,
  CY-BOCS) are all present and correct.

## Summary

Overall coverage quality for Child & Adolescent Psychiatry is **strong** — markedly stronger than
what the original Adult Psychiatry gap analysis found, with no entirely-missing subsections and no
missing named trials or tables. The seven concrete gaps identified (the non-organic
failure-to-thrive / signs-of-abuse checklist, the head-injury-25% ADHD figure, dysthymic disorder,
the 12%-adolescent-suicide/Hawton-1986 figure, the attempters-vs-completers psychiatric-disorder
contrast, the Silver 1996 encopresis audit finding, and the Dalton et al. 1989 hospitalisation
list) are all narrow, well-defined additions rather than evidence of systemic thinness — most of
this topic's content is already present, correctly cited, and numerically verified against the
book. The single highest-priority item to add is the signs-of-abuse/neglect/failure-to-thrive
checklist in Section 2, since it is the one genuine multi-item table-shaped loss (classic EMI
material) rather than a single dropped sentence; the rest are individually low-effort additions
(one paragraph or trap each).
