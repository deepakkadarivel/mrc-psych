# Gap Analysis: mrc-psych-lake (web) vs SPMM Source PDF
## Learning Disability — What's Missing, From an Exam Standpoint

**Purpose of this document:** compares the live web page for Learning Disability
(`content/study-guides/learning-disability.json`) against the full extracted text of the
source book (`content/notes/learning-disability.json`, i.e. `resources/paper-b/books/13-learning-disability.pdf`)
to find condensed-notes gaps the way the earlier Adult Psychiatry review did. Unlike that
review, this topic's web page turns out to be in genuinely strong shape: every named
subsection, every named study, and every named genetic syndrome in the 28-page book has a
corresponding block on the page, and the guide has clearly also been cross-checked against
the question bank (14 explicit `gap` blocks disclose facts the questions test that the book
doesn't cover, each correctly labelled "Not covered in books/13-learning-disability.pdf").
The findings below are therefore narrower than a typical "whole sections missing" report:
a handful of specific omitted facts, one date discrepancy, and — the most important finding —
several places where the guide states a more specific or wider-ranging fact than the cited
book page actually supports. That last category is flagged in its own section because it's
the same failure mode the zero-hallucination rule exists to catch: a citation that points to
a real page, attached to a claim that page doesn't actually make.

---

## Section-by-Section Gap Findings

### 1. Definitions & Classification (book p.2)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing:**
- The book states the developmental-period cutoff differently for the two classification
  systems: **"presentation in early life (within the developmental period which DSM stipulates
  as up to 18 years of age but is not specified by ICD)."** The web page's opening paragraph
  ("**LD**... is defined by an IQ below 70 presenting within the developmental period...")
  drops this DSM-vs-ICD distinction entirely. This is exactly the kind of "two systems disagree
  on a specific number" fact MRCPsych favours for a distractor, and it's currently invisible on
  the page.
- The book opens by listing three near-synonymous terms in current use — "learning disability",
  "developmental disability", and "intellectual disability" — plus the separate but often-confused
  "learning difficulty" (used for dyslexia/dyspraxia specifically). The web page's terminology
  paragraph covers ICD-10/DSM-5/ICD-11 naming and the LD-vs-mental-retardation-vs-intellectual-
  disability equivalence, but never states the book's own worked distinction that "learning
  difficulty" ≠ "learning disability" (the closest thing on the page is an unrelated trap about
  ICD-11 and dyslexia, sourced separately from the question bank, not this book passage).

### 2. Epidemiology (book p.3)
**Status: fully covered.** Prevalence (1-3%), male:female ratio (~1.5:1), severity-distribution
pie-chart figures (85/10/4/1-2%), comorbid seizure rate (~16%, up to 50% in profound LD),
hearing loss (25-42%), and the CIPOLD premature-mortality figures (13/20 years) are all present
and numerically match the book. No gaps found.

### 3. Aetiology & Prevention (book pp.4-5)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing:**
- The book's **primary prevention** list runs to seven numbered items, but the page's condensing
  paragraph only reflects items 1-3 (immunisation against rubella/measles-encephalitis/bacterial
  meningitis/TB; periconceptual folic acid; good antenatal/perinatal/neonatal care). The book's
  items **6 ("Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders" — i.e. avoiding alcohol in pregnancy) and 7
  ("Accidents in the home and traffic accidents" — i.e. preventing head injury)** are genuine
  primary-prevention measures in the source text and are absent from the page's prevention
  block entirely. This matters because the page's own Behavioural Phenotypes section discusses
  FASD in detail as an *aetiology* but the *prevention* angle on it is never connected back to
  this section's three-tier prevention framework.

### 4. Subcultural vs Biological/Pathological LD (book pp.5-6)
**Status: fully covered.** E. O. Lewis (1933), the six-row subcultural-vs-biological comparison
table (severity, social class distribution, family IQ, dysmorphic characters, syndromic
features, behavioural phenotypes), and the "later epidemiological studies have not supported
this division" caveat are all present and match the book verbatim. No gaps found.

### 5. Social History, Normalisation & Criminal Justice (book pp.7-8)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing:**
- The book opens this section with historical context — late-19th-century social exclusion,
  people with LD being labelled "feeble minded" or "idiots" and blamed for crime/poverty, leading
  to institutional confinement. None of this appears on the page, which begins directly with
  Nirje/1960s Scandinavia. Lower yield than the numeric gaps above, but this kind of
  "history of the specialty" framing does appear in MRCPsych Paper B.
- **Date discrepancy to verify against the source PDF directly:** the book text names **two
  different years** for "Valuing People" — "(2001)" when defining LD for health/social care
  purposes, and a separate **"(2007)"** when describing the "key four principles of rights,
  independence, choice and inclusion." The web page's paragraph merges these into one sentence
  ("Valuing People (2001)... the same policy framework later set out four key principles..."),
  which reads as if both facts come from the single 2001 document. Since the real-world "Valuing
  People" white paper was published in 2001 (with "Valuing People Now" following in 2009), the
  book's own "(2007)" citation may itself be a typo — but as extracted, the two sources disagree,
  and the page currently smooths over rather than surfaces that disagreement. Worth a manual
  check against the PDF page image before trusting either date in an answer.

### 6. Psychiatric Comorbidity — Epidemiology, Diagnostic Difficulties & Assessment Tools (book p.9)
**Status: fully covered.** Deb et al (2001a), Hastings et al (2004) figures (46.3% life events,
32.2% vs 11.2% psychiatric illness, 6% vs 37% no physical illness, 22.1% epilepsy, 19% vs 8%
poor eyesight, 28.8%/23.6% overweight/obese), the seven-disorder prevalence table, diagnostic
overshadowing (Emerson & Baines 2010), and the PAS-ADD/Mini PAS-ADD/PAS-ADD Checklist tool
table are all present and numerically match. No gaps found.

### 7. Psychiatric Syndromes — Schizophrenia, Bipolar, Depression, Anxiety (book pp.9-11)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing:**
- The book states a specific presentation for **moderate** LD that the page doesn't carry: "For
  people with moderate LD, hallucinations may occur and in combination with persecutory
  feelings, may lead to fear, social withdrawal and aggressive outburst." The page's schizophrenia
  paragraph only contrasts "severe LD" (thought disorder/delusions less evident, aggression/
  bizarre behaviour dominate) — the moderate-LD-specific hallucination-plus-persecutory-feelings
  picture is a distinct EMI-style differentiator between severity bands and isn't reproduced.
- Usher's syndrome, velo-cardio-facial syndrome (Goldberg et al 1993), and Prader-Willi/chromosome
  15 maternal UPD (Boer et al 2002) are all correctly captured in the "genetic syndromes
  associated with psychosis" table — no gap there.

### 8. Autism/PDD, ADHD, Conduct Disorder & Dementia in Down's Syndrome (book pp.11-12)
**Status: fully covered**, and in fact the page goes further than the book here (five
explicitly-labelled `gap` blocks add autism heritability/sibling-recurrence figures, obesity
prevalence in ASD, savant syndrome, and HFA-vs-Asperger's — all correctly disclosed as
question-bank-only content, not attributed to this book). No missing book content found.

### 9. Challenging Behaviour, Self-Injurious Behaviour & Pica (book pp.12-14)
**Status: fully covered.** Emerson's (1995) definition, the three-tier clinically-significant-
behaviour criteria, predisposing factors, prevalence figures (7%/14% challenging behaviour,
20-44% behaviour disorders), Borthwick-Duffy (1994) SIB figures by setting, the Wiseley et al
(2002) endogenous-opioid hypothesis, the SIB-associated-syndrome list (Gates, 2003), and the
pica figures (McAdam et al 2004) all match the book. No gaps found.

### 10. Psychopharmacology in Learning Disability (book pp.14-15)
**Status: fully covered.** Every drug class in the book (antipsychotics, antidepressants,
anticonvulsants, lithium plus its EEG/renal/cell-count baseline requirement and toxicity risk
citing Frith Prescribing Guidelines p.99, stimulants, opiate antagonists, anti-libidinal drugs,
beta-blockers) and the three dysphagia mechanisms are all present, plus a summary drug-to-
indication table. No gaps found.

### 11. Psychological Treatments (book p.15)
**Status: fully covered.** The Nagel and Leiper survey figures (80%/35%/17%), the Sturmey (2004)
CBT evidence base from forensic secure settings, and the behavioural-treatment/ABC-chart/family-
therapy content all match the book. No gaps found.

### 12. Down Syndrome (book pp.16-17)
**Status: reasonably covered but missing:**
- The book's clinical-features list for the head/neck includes **"narrowed hypo pharynx, maxilla
  reduced more than mandible, underdeveloped bridge of nose, eyes close together"** — the page's
  paragraph lists the higher-yield items (oblique palpebral fissures, flat nasal bridge,
  brachycephaly, high-arched palate, protruding tongue, atlanto-axial instability, Brushfield
  spots, epicanthic folds, low-set ears) but drops these four. Low individual yield but worth
  noting as the one incomplete dysmorphology list in an otherwise very thorough syndrome writeup.
- The book's congenital-defects list includes **umbilical and inguinal hernia** among the GI
  abnormalities; the page's paragraph names oesophageal atresia and Hirschsprung disease but not
  the hernias.
- Maternal-age risk table, trisomy/translocation/mosaicism percentages (92-95% / 2-4% / 1-2.5%),
  IQ trajectory, age-banded dementia-prevalence table, epilepsy figures (10%/40%/80%), and cause-
  of-death figures are all otherwise fully and accurately covered.

### 13. Sex Chromosome Disorders (book pp.17-19)
**Status: fully covered** for every figure the book states (Fragile X incidence/penetrance/
CGG-repeat mechanism, Turner/Triple X/Klinefelter/XYY incidence and features) — **but see the
Fragile X fabrication flagged in the "Citation-Accuracy Concerns" section below**, since two of
this section's specific clinical claims are not actually supported by the cited book page.

### 14. Autosomal Deletion/Duplication Syndromes (book pp.19-22)
**Status: fully covered** for Prader-Willi, Angelman, Williams, Cri-du-chat, and Smith-Magenis —
every figure checked (M:F ratios, incidence ranges, deletion loci, parent-of-origin, epilepsy/
hyperphagia/IQ percentages) matches the book precisely, including the correctly-preserved
"majority paternal origin" detail for Cri-du-chat (85% de novo) that would be easy to
mis-state as maternal by analogy with Angelman. **See the DiGeorge/velo-cardio-facial prevalence
discrepancy flagged below** — that is this section's one numeric problem.

### 15. Autosomal Dominant Conditions (book pp.22-23)
**Status: fully covered.** Tuberous sclerosis (seizure rate 90%, LD-severity spectrum, ash-leaf
spots), NF1 (all seven NIH diagnostic criteria, verbal-IQ>performance-IQ detail), and
Sturge-Weber (port-wine stain/meningeal angioma triad) all match the book. No gaps found.

### 16. Autosomal Recessive, X-linked & Miscellaneous Causes (book pp.23-26)
**Status: reasonably covered but see the Laurence-Moon-Biedl fabrication flagged below** — that
is the one problem in an otherwise complete section. PKU, Hurler syndrome, Sanfilippo disease,
Lesch-Nyhan syndrome (HGPRT deficiency, self-injury timeline, cause of death), Hunter syndrome
(life-expectancy-with-vs-without-LD figures), Cornelia de Lange syndrome (NIPBL gene, small
hands/feet), FASD (UK incidence ~1/100, 6,000-7,000 babies/year), and congenital hypothyroidism
are all otherwise fully and accurately covered.

### 17. Epilepsy & Learning Disability (book p.27)
**Status: fully covered.** LD-in-epilepsy and epilepsy-in-LD prevalence figures, syndrome-specific
epilepsy rates (Down's 5-10%, Fragile X 20-25% resembling benign Rolandic epilepsy, Angelman
>90%, Rett 90%, tuberous sclerosis 90%), the neuronal-ceroid-lipofuscinoses/metabolic-cause list,
infantile spasms/West syndrome (onset, Moro-like flexion pattern, hypsarrhythmia EEG), and
Lennox-Gastaut syndrome are all present and numerically match the book. No gaps found.

## Citation-Accuracy Concerns (facts attached to a real book-page citation that the book doesn't actually state)

These are more serious than an omission, because a reader has no way to tell them apart from
genuine book content — the citation looks identical either way. Each was checked directly
against the corresponding block's full text in `content/notes/learning-disability.json`.

1. **DiGeorge/velo-cardio-facial syndrome prevalence widened beyond the source.** The web page
   states "prevalence estimated around **1/2,000-1/4,000**" (Autosomal Deletion/Duplication
   Syndromes section, cited to `books/13-learning-disability.pdf` p.16). The book's actual text
   says only **"Prevalence estimated to be 1/4000"** — a single figure, no range, and no "1/2,000"
   anywhere in the source. The 1/4,000 figure itself is correctly reproduced elsewhere on the same
   page ("occurs in around 1 in 4,000 people"), which makes the inconsistency within the study
   guide's own two paragraphs on the same syndrome easy to spot once flagged.
2. **Laurence-Moon-Biedl syndrome: "central obesity" is not in the book.** The web page's
   paragraph lists "Retinitis pigmentosa, polydactyly (extra digits), spastic paraplegia, night
   blindness (red cone atrophy), hypogonadism, NIDDM, renal problems, **central obesity**;
   associated with mild-moderate LD" (cited to `books/13-learning-disability.pdf` p.16). The
   book's actual sentence is: "Clinical features include retinitis pigmentosa, extra digits
   (polydactyly), spastic paraplegia, night blindness (due to red cone atrophy), hypogonadism,
   NIDDM and renal problems. Associated with mild and moderate LD." — obesity is never mentioned.
   (Obesity is a well-known real-world feature of this syndrome, now usually called Bardet-Biedl
   syndrome, but it is not something *this* source text says, and the citation implies it is.)
3. **Fragile X: two added specifics not in the cited book page.** The web page's Fragile X
   clinical-features paragraph states speech/language delay presents as "**palilalia, echolalia,
   cluttering, over-detailed speech**" (cited to `books/13-learning-disability.pdf` p.16). The
   book only says "speech and language delays" with no named sub-features. Separately, the page's
   Fragile-X-and-IQ paragraph adds that "the drop correlates with **FMR1 methylation status**"
   (same citation) — the book's own explanation for the IQ-drop phenomenon is entirely different
   ("may be related to higher stress upon abstract abilities in different IQ tests used for
   adults and adolescents compared to children"); methylation status is never mentioned in the
   source text at all.

These three should either be re-cited to a real source (question bank / Oxford Handbook / other
already-committed corpus file, if the fact appears there) or removed — as written they carry the
same authority as a verified book citation while stating something the book doesn't say.

## Overall Coverage Verdict

This is the strongest topic reviewed under this gap-analysis exercise so far. Every one of the
book's ~19 named subsections and all 24 named genetic syndromes have a corresponding, generally
accurate block on the web page, all major named studies (Deb et al 2001a, Hastings et al 2004,
Wiseley et al 2002, Boer et al 2002, Goldberg et al 1993, Borthwick-Duffy 1994, Gates 2003,
Sturmey 2004, Nagel and Leiper, McAdam et al 2004) are present, and the page has clearly already
been cross-checked against the question bank, with 14 honestly-labelled `gap` blocks disclosing
exam-tested facts the book itself doesn't cover. The remaining book-vs-page gaps are narrow: two
missing primary-prevention items, a missing DSM-vs-ICD developmental-period distinction, a
missing moderate-LD psychosis presentation detail, a couple of missing dysmorphology/hernia
details in Down syndrome, historical context in the social-history section, and an unresolved
Valuing People date discrepancy — collectively no more than 4-5 new blocks' worth of content.
The one thing worth prioritising over all of the above is the **three citation-accuracy findings**
(DiGeorge prevalence, Laurence-Moon-Biedl obesity, Fragile X speech-features/FMR1 methylation):
these are small in volume but are exactly the failure mode the project's zero-hallucination rule
exists to catch, since they carry a real page citation while stating something that page does not.
